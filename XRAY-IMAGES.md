# X-ray images for the Reading X-rays guide

Research only, 11 Nov 2026. Nothing in the app was changed and no image was saved in this folder. This file lists real radiographs that may lawfully be shown in a public repository and web app, with where each comes from, and the openly licensed sources that name the normal structures on each film. It is for the entry `xray-guide` (`src/parts/data13.js`).

## How this was done, and its limits

- **Where the films come from.** Almost all are on Wikimedia Commons. Each file's licence, author, source and pixel size were read from its own Commons page through the Commons API on 11 Nov 2026. The licence column gives the short licence name the API returns for that page (for example "CC BY-SA 3.0" for "Creative Commons Attribution-Share Alike 3.0 Unported"). A few older files are also offered under the GNU Free Documentation License; only the Creative Commons licence is listed.
- **What was looked at.** Every radiograph in the tables was viewed (the Gray drawings in section 3 were not opened), but at reduced size (about 500 to 960 pixels wide, some smaller in a grid). Side markers and large text were read. Small text can be missed at that size. **Before a film goes into the app, open it at full size and check its edges and corners once more.**
- **"Normal" and the finding shown** are taken from the file's own page, in quotation marks where quoted. They are not this file's reading of the film. German descriptions are given in English.
- **Rejected on sight:** a name, number, date or hospital label on the picture; screenshots of a viewer; "no derivatives" licences; pages with no licence. Radiopaedia, Radiology Masterclass, NICE, RCR and textbooks were not used.
- **Side markers.** Many films carry a side marker (R, L, Dx, Sin) and some carry two or three letters beside it, which look like radiographer markers. They are noted in the tables so that Krishna can decide; cropping removes them.
- **Wikimedia limits automated downloads.** Fetch any chosen file once, at one of the standard thumbnail widths or the original, not in bulk.

Licences in the tables: **CC0** is the CC0 1.0 public domain dedication (https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). **Public domain** is stated on the page with its reason. **CC BY** needs credit. **CC BY-SA** needs credit and the same licence on the edited picture (section 5).

## 1. Films that may be used: public domain, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA

Columns: the view; normal, or the finding shown (from the file's page); the file's page; the direct file; author; licence as stated; the attribution line the licence requires; pixel size of the original; patient marking visible; quality.

For CC0 and public domain films no credit is required; the line gives a courtesy credit. For CC BY and CC BY-SA films the line is written for a film that the app has drawn on: keep the title, author, licence name and link, the source link, and the note of changes.

## 3. Sources for the anatomy labels

Rule kept: no structure name below comes from memory. Each list is what the named source itself prints or writes. Where no source names a structure the guide mentions, that is said, so that the label is not drawn until a source is found.

Three kinds of source were found:

1. **Labelled radiographs on Commons** (CC0 or CC BY-SA): few, and good for the chest, wrist, hand and foot.
2. **Wikipedia article text** (footer: "Text is available under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 License; additional terms may apply"): names the bony landmarks in words, not on a film. Each list below is what one automated read of the article returned on 11 Nov 2026; open the article to confirm each name before it is drawn. The Knee and Elbow reads stopped before the footer, which is the same on every Wikipedia article.
3. **Gray's Anatomy, 1918 plates on Commons** (public domain): engravings of each bone with printed labels. They are listed with links, but **their labels were not transcribed here**, and they use 1918 names. Read the plate before citing it.

## 5. What a CC BY-SA film requires of the app

Read from the licence text at https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en and https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/legalcode.en on 11 Nov 2026. Each film keeps the version on its own page.

- **Credit, on or beside the film (4.0, Section 3(a)(1)).** Keep "identification of the creator(s)", "a notice that refers to this Public License" and "a URI or hyperlink to the Licensed Material", "indicate if You modified the Licensed Material", and "include the text of, or the URI or hyperlink to, this Public License". Section 3(a)(2) lets this be done "in any reasonable manner", for example "by providing a URI or hyperlink to a resource that includes the required information": a credit line under the film that opens a credits page will do. Version 3.0 asks the same in Section 4(c) ("the name of the Original Author", "the title of the Work if supplied", "a credit identifying the use of the Work in the Adaptation"), and Section 3(b) asks that an adaptation "clearly label, demarcate or otherwise identify that changes were made".
- **Share-alike, for the outlined film only (4.0, Section 3(b)).** A film with outlines and labels drawn on it is "Adapted Material". "The Adapter's License You apply must be a Creative Commons license with the same License Elements, this version or later, or a BY-SA Compatible License", its text or link must be given, and no extra terms or "Effective Technological Measures" may restrict it. In 3.0, Section 4(b) says an adaptation may be distributed "only under" the terms it lists, the first being "the terms of this License". So each outlined CC BY-SA film must itself be offered under CC BY-SA, with a notice saying so. If the outline is kept as a separate layer over an unchanged film, the film is still shown with credit and licence, and it is safest to treat the pair as adapted.
- **What it does not ask.** Share-alike attaches to the adapted picture, not to the pages around it: 3.0 says "a work that constitutes a Collection will not be considered an Adaptation". The guide's text, code and its own drawings keep their own terms. Since the repository is public, the credits and licence notices must be in the repository as well as in the app. CC0 and public domain films need none of this, which is why the CC0 series is the first choice for every normal film.
