Every pathway and rule, step by step

Patients move one way

MTS level 4 and 5 patients, from triage to discharge

  1. TriageMTS level 4 or 5
  2. 2Secondary triageECG, irrigation, dressing, only if needed
  3. You, the MOFill in the green slip, attach the queue number
  4. 4Red BoxThe slip goes in; the patient waits in the tent
  5. Treatment room (PPP)Calls the number, does each order, documents it

Results pending, observation or referral

PPP takes the patient to the CCA (Chair Centric Area) and hands over to Yellow Zone Extension staff, who record vital signs on the slip and send the patient back to you.

To finish

Results back: call the queue number again. Attach the slip to the BHT, complete the notes, give a TCA from the appointment flow chart and discharge advice.

Three rules to keep

Never waits outside

Bring them inside

ElderlyNSTEMI or ACSStat drugs and monitoring
  • Seat them in the CCA, the rooms in front of R2, the R2 corridor or any zone with space
  • The designated MA and HO there start management and monitoring
  • No space: tell the EP in charge

Admit, discharge, transfer

Reassess first

  • Reassess every patient; review every blood result and image
  • Came within 24 hours: ask the TL, a master student or the EP to look at the case
  • CT brain and going home: EP review, then an EMWATCH or EMACC appointment

When it stalls

Call the EP in charge

  • Several disciplines and no disposition after a long wait: inform the EP
  • External referral: discuss with the EP before accepting
  • Pending at the end of your shift: hand over to the next MO and document who

Four more rules

One-way flow: no repeats after secondary triage

  • Do not repeat the ECG, dressing or vital signs later unless they were missed
  • An ECG you ask for in the Green Zone is done in the Procedure Room: do not send the patient back to secondary triage

Sources: HKL ED one-way procedure flow (Green Zone); KKM MTS announcement, Jul 2026.

Down-triage: the EP allows it

  • Examine the patient properly first, then document it on the triage form

Source: memo HKL/JK/98/188/3(22)22, 4 Oct 2022. EP rule: to be confirmed.

IV antibiotics when no bed is free

  • Look for a bed first in Yellow Obs, ISO or YZ Ext, and pass the patient over to the zone in-charge
  • If none is free, the patient can have IV antibiotics on a chair in the CCA, with one of the four allowed antibiotics only: Augmentin, cefuroxime, ceftriaxone or Unasyn

Source: IV antibiotic memo, 30 Jun 2026.

When the primary team is delayed

  • The EP may decide the admission within 4 hours if the primary team is delayed

Sources: KKM MTS announcement, Jul 2026; memo HKL/JK/98/188/3(17)23, 1 Sep 2023.

Asthma Zone

  1. 1
    Triage level given at primary triage?
    Level 2 or 3: Critical zone, R1
  2. 2
    Level 4 or 5 Asthma card and a 5000 queue number
  3. 3
    Asthma Treatment Room (PPP)
    • Secondary assessment; uptriage at once if unstable
    • Vital signs and PEFR before and after treatment
    • Reassess at 15–20 minutes
  4. 4
    Resolved after the inhaler cycle?
    Not resolved: Uptriage to R1
  5. 5
    Resolved Health education, then the card comes to your room
  6. 6
    You re-examine the patient. Stable?
    Not stable: Uptriage to R1
  7. Stable Discharge (you, the Green Zone MO)
    • Give the balance of the MDI the patient used, with the spacer
    • Prednisolone 30 mg once daily for 5 days
    • Salbutamol MDI 4 puffs every 4–6 hours for 1 day, then 2 puffs as needed
    • Do not share the MDI; bring your own spacer next time; come back at once (TCA stat) if the attack recurs

Source: Asthma memo, Nov 2025. Discharge prescription: department BA/COPD protocol, 27 Mar 2020.

Refer to

  • Joint dislocation, shoulder too: the on-call Orthopaedic team, not the clinic.
  • GI bleed: medical cause, Gastroenterology; surgical cause, General Surgery. Cholecystitis without jaundice: General Surgery. Jaundice: Gastroenterology.
  • Medical ward: refer to the Medical specialist or MO on call first.
  • Psychiatric relapse, known history, no other medical problem: Psychiatry MO on call, no bloods or CT first.
  • Gout: nearest Klinik Kesihatan; complex cases to Rheumatology.
  • Dengue from a KK, warning signs only, normal CCTVR and vitals, platelets over 50,000, not morbidly obese: straight to the Observation Ward.
  • IPR (respiratory): stable, no inotropes, Venturi 40% at most.

Sources: the ebook above; EMACC SOP (1 Feb 2018: check the extension and hours are current); Asthma memo, 13 Nov 2025; department BA/COPD protocol, 27 Mar 2020; referral memos and letters 2018 to 2026. A reference, not a substitute for clinical judgement. No patient data.
