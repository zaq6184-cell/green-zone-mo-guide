## (b) Points inside the CPG: where its algorithm, text and tables disagree or leave a gap

None is resolved in the draft. Those that change an ED decision are in the node's `gap` or `info`.

**The score and the algorithm**

1. **McIsaac is described twice, differently.** The prose (p3) gives fever above 38°C, tonsillar swelling or exudates, and swollen anterior cervical nodes. The algorithm box (p12, read at 600 dpi) prints the fever item as "> = 38⁰ C" (greater than or equal), tonsillar exudates only, and tender anterior cervical nodes. A temperature of exactly 38.0°C scores under the algorithm but not under the prose. The draft follows the algorithm and shows the prose wording in `ms` info.
2. **Age.** The prose says the score uses age but gives no rule. The algorithm gives a point for age <15 and nothing for any other age; the CPG prints no band for older adults. The draft says so and adds nothing.
3. **Two branches without a rule between them.** From a history of sore throat the algorithm runs a viral-features box (rhinitis, hoarseness, cough, conjunctivitis, diarrhoea, oropharyngeal ulcers) and the McIsaac score side by side (p12). It does not say which wins when they disagree, for example hoarseness in a child with fever, exudate and tender nodes (score 4). The draft asks the viral question first, as the left branch, and its viral outcome links back to the score.
4. **Score 4 or more.** The algorithm allows either a swab or an antibiotic (p12) and gives no way to choose. The prose (p9) allows an antibiotic without a result only when a streptococcal throat is suspected, the patient looks toxic and follow-up is not possible. Those three are joined by "and"; "toxic-looking" is not defined; and the prose does not tie them to the score.
5. **A negative RADT.** The algorithm's "GAS detected: no" arrow goes straight to symptomatic treatment (p12), but the text (p7) asks for a culture to confirm a negative RADT whenever possible.
6. **Waiting for the culture.** The swab route has no step for a pending result. Culture takes 48 hours (p6); starting treatment up to 9 days late keeps the benefit against rheumatic fever (p7), which supports waiting; but the CPG never says how the patient is recalled. (The laboratory text, p5, reads plates at 18 to 24 hours and re-examines negatives at 48 hours.)
7. **Swab site.** The posterior pharyngeal wall is to be swabbed, yet the oropharynx is listed among sites to avoid (p3); the CPG does not explain the distinction.
8. **RADT** is said to be available locally (p6) but not evaluated locally (p7): not a contradiction, but it means a negative RADT cannot stand alone.

**Antibiotics**

9. **One penicillin V dose for every child:** 250mg bid or tid x 10 days (p9), with no weight or age bands. The CPG never defines the paediatric age group; the only age cut-off anywhere is the score's <15.
10. **Benzathine penicillin by weight:** <27kg and >27kg (p9). Exactly 27 kg is not covered. No adult dose is printed, although IM benzathine penicillin is named (Evidence level I) as an option for recurrence (p9).
11. **Maximums:** erythromycin estolate and ethylsuccinate carry max. 1g/day; clindamycin 20-30 mg/kg/day tid has no maximum (p9).
12. **Adults beyond penicillin:** erythromycin is given as four times or twice daily with no dose in mg (p10); the first generation cephalosporin is not named and has no dose; clindamycin has no adult dose. None states a course length, though 5.2 sets a full 10 days for any oral course (p9).
13. **Grades for the two adult penicillin V regimens differ:** 250 mg t.i.d. or q.i.d is Evidence level II-1, 500 mg b.i.d is level III (p10). The child dose is ungraded.
14. **Recurrence** (p9) sits in the children's section; amoxycillin-clavulanate and cefuroxime are listed without doses; adults are not covered.
15. **Response time:** children usually respond within 24-48 hours (p9); the algorithm's reassessment after 48 hours (p12) covers everyone.

**Symptomatic care**

16. **Ibuprofen and NSAIDs:** small studies graded Evidence level I found ibuprofen safe and effective, yet routine NSAIDs are not recommended because of gastrointestinal bleeding; that sentence carries no grade or reference (p8).
17. **Dexamethasone** is graded Evidence level 1 (printed with the digit, not the Roman I) on one small study, with a call for more studies and no dose (p8).
18. **No doses** for paracetamol or ibuprofen anywhere.

**Complications and surgery**

19. **No red flags.** There are no signs of a peritonsillar or retropharyngeal abscess, no airway or toxicity signs, no admission criteria and no intravenous antibiotic. Acute management of an abscess is limited to accepted surgical practice that every abscess is drained (p11), plus the two quinsy rules for tonsillectomy (p11). The CPG names no team.
20. **Tonsillectomy threshold.** The CPG's own criterion is more than 6 episodes over a 12-month period (p11, printed "> 6"), while the two guidelines it cites set more than 5 (SIGN) and more than 3 (American Academy of Otolaryngology) episodes (p10); it does not explain its choice. Its third criterion, symptoms over a 12-month period, can be read as symptoms lasting more than a year or as episodes within a year.
21. **Diphtheria and gonococcal pharyngitis** are named as the exceptions where antibiotics help (p8) and appear in Table 1 at <1% (p2), with no management, isolation or notification advice.

**Wording and references**

22. **Citation numbers drift from the list.** The McIsaac score is cited as reference 22, but McIsaac is number 23 in the list; Table 1's source is cited as 14 but listed as 15; SIGN is cited as 47 but listed as 48; the sentence on antibiotics for symptom relief (p8) cites 57, which is the dexamethasone study; the recurrence options (p9) cite numbers one below the matching titles. The list itself numbers two references 69; and number 73 has authors but no title while 74 has a title (a BMJ 2000 trial of penicillin course length) but no authors, which looks like one reference split in two.
23. **Spellings as printed:** McIssac once (p3), diptheriae (Table 1, p2, and p8), gonorrhoea in Table 1 but gonorrhea on p8; American spellings throughout (etiology, diarrhea, pediatric). The draft uses UK spelling in its own words and writes diphtheria and gonococcal infection in place of the misprinted organism names.
